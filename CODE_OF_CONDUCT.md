# Code of Conduct

RotaPress welcomes contributors and users of all backgrounds and experience
levels. Everyone should be able to participate without harassment or discrimination.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful, patient and considerate. Volunteers have different schedules,
  communication styles and technical experience.
- Discuss ideas and behavior constructively. Explain disagreements without
  attacking a person's character or identity.
- Accept feedback, acknowledge mistakes and help repair their effects.
- Respect privacy, consent and requests to stop unwanted contact.
- Make space for newcomers and people whose perspectives differ from your own.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment, threats, discriminatory language, sexual attention or imagery,
personal insults, sustained disruption and intimidation are not acceptable.
Do not publish private information without consent, pursue someone after being
asked to stop, or retaliate against someone for making or supporting a report.

## Scope

This policy applies to project issues, pull requests, discussions, events and
other community spaces. It also applies when someone represents RotaPress in
public, and to related private conduct that affects community participation.
Maintainers are subject to the same standards as other participants.

## Report a concern

Email **[abuse@rotaract.lu](mailto:abuse@rotaract.lu)** privately. Describe what
happened, where and when it happened, and relevant links or evidence that you can
safely share. Include any immediate concern and a way to contact you if desired.
Do not put incident details or another person's private information in a public issue.

Reports are handled as confidentially as practical. Information is shared only
with people needed to assess the report and take action; absolute confidentiality
cannot be promised. You do not need to confront the person involved before reporting.

If the report concerns a maintainer, say so. That person should not decide their
own case; another uninvolved maintainer should handle it where available.
Retaliation for a good-faith report is itself a violation.

## Enforcement

Maintainers assess context, severity, impact and repeated behavior. They may remove
content, request a correction, issue a private warning, restrict participation
temporarily or exclude someone permanently. Serious threats or harassment can
result in immediate exclusion without an earlier warning.

When practical, maintainers explain the decision and conditions for returning.
To request reconsideration or provide new information, write to the same address;
an uninvolved maintainer should review the request where available. Enforcement
should protect affected people and be proportionate and consistent.
